# `mbalit` — Pitch Script

**Tone:** calm, low, conversational. Slow down on stats and on names. Pause whenever a scene transition lands — let the visuals breathe.

**Total runtime target:** ~8 minutes (3 min Act I · 2 min Act II · 3 min Act III + close)

---

## ACT I — The problem (≈3 minutes)

### Scene 1 · The fields *(~18s)*
> "Right now, while we're sitting in this room — there are children working in The Gambia's rubbish.
> Not as a metaphor. Today. This morning. On the Bakoteh dumpsite, ten minutes from where most of us live."

*(Pause. Let the photo sit.)*

---

### Scene 2 · Problem 1 of 4 — Water bodies *(~22s — the May 2017 beat)*
> "Problem one — they dump it in our rivers.
> In May 2017, the Bolong Fenyo lagoon at Gunjur turned blood-red overnight. Fish kill. Bird die-off. An entire ecosystem suffocated.
> When the lab came back: nitrates were forty times the safe level. Arsenic, double the WHO limit. And half the wells in Bakoteh have fecal coliform above 100 CFU."

*(Tap the stat cards as they land.)*

---

### Scene 3 · Problem 2 of 4 — Streets *(~16s)*
> "Problem two — they dump it in our streets.
> About ninety percent of the country's waste is never treated. Only ten percent is even collected. The first hard rain turns the road into a sewer. The next dry week turns it into a mosquito nursery."

---

### Scene 4 · Problem 3 of 4 — Open burning *(~16s)*
> "Problem three — and then they set it on fire.
> Bakoteh receives four hundred and sixty tonnes a day. Almost eighteen hectares, mostly burning. The smoke reaches schools, clinics, and an SOS Children's Village downwind."

---

### Scene 5 · Problem 4 of 4 — Forests & wildlife *(~16s)*
> "Problem four — they dump it in our forests.
> Last December, multi-agency clean-ups pulled illegal dumps out of Nyambai and Kabafita — water catchments protecting ten community boreholes. And at Brufut, cattle now graze on plastic."

---

### Scene 6 · The human cost *(~22s)*
> "And it lands on the same families twice.
> Once — when their children scavenge instead of going to school. A full bag of metal pays fifteen pence. Fifteen.
> And again — when the contaminated water and the smoke send those same children to a clinic. Ninety-three percent of water samples adjacent to Bakoteh exceed the safe coliform limit."

---

### Scene 7 · Neneh's story *(~28s — slow down here)*
> "This is Neneh.
> She was twelve. The third of nine children. She had not been to school in four years.
> She worked the Bakoteh dump alongside her siblings — fifteen pence a bag.
>
> *(Beat.)*
>
> I personally do not believe that any child should have to start working before they've had a basic education.
>
> In 2015, Child Aid Gambia sponsored her into school. By 2017, she was scoring A grades.
> Today she lives away from the dump. She never has to scavenge again.
>
> Neneh is why we built this."

---

### Scene 8 · The voices *(~22s — read each name slowly)*
*(The five testimonies will scroll. Read the names; let the quotes land on screen.)*

> "These people have names.
>
> **Tida Sillah. Saffiatou Sambou. Adama Ida Ceesay. Mariama Nyassi. Ndey Ngum.**
>
> Mothers. Landlords. Waste pickers. They've told their story to Voice Gambia, to The Point, to NGO field workers. Nobody has built them anything.
>
> So — we will."

---

### Scene 9 · The vision *(~20s — the industry argument)*
> "What if we turn this into an industry.
>
> Because this is how industries actually happen. You cannot talk about recycling, you cannot talk about processing plastic, you cannot talk about any of it — until you have a system that reliably *collects* the waste in the first place.
>
> Collect. Then sort. Then recycle. Then goods.
>
> That is what `mbalit` is for."

*(Pause for the depth-blur transition into Act II.)*

---

## ACT II — The product (≈2 minutes — ~12s per feature)

### Scene 10 · Feature 01 · Phone + PIN onboarding
> "No SMS. No verification code. Just a phone number and a four-digit PIN. Twelve seconds — and you have an account."

### Scene 11 · Feature 02 · Choose what you have
> "Household. Garden. Renovation. E-waste. The driver knows what they're picking up before they get there."

### Scene 12 · Feature 03 · Transparent pricing
> "The price ticks up live as you add buckets. No haggling at the gate."

### Scene 13 · Feature 04 · Plus Code pickup
> "Greater Banjul is full of compounds with no formal address. A Plus Code is a ten-by-ten metre square anyone with a phone can resolve."

### Scene 14 · Feature 05 · Live broadcast
> "The job goes out to every nearby driver — instantly."

### Scene 15 · Feature 06 · Race condition, solved
> "Two drivers tap *Accept* in the same second — only one gets the job. Atomic single-winner write. No double-booking. Ever."

### Scene 16 · Feature 07 · Live tracking
> "From the moment they accept, you watch them on the map. They watch you. Both phones, same truth."

### Scene 17 · Feature 08 · Mobile-money checkout
> "Wave. AfriMoney. QMoney. Yonna Wallet — the four wallets that already move money in The Gambia. The driver gets paid before they drive away."

### Scene 18 · Feature 09 · Hazard reporting
> "See illegal dumping? One photo. One tap. KMC gets it the moment it lands — geo-tagged, timestamped, undeniable."

### Scene 19 · Feature 10 · Authority dashboard
> "The Kanifing Municipal Council sees every report in real time. Live map. Live counts. They can act on the same day — not the same quarter."

## ACT III — The handoff & the close

### Scene 20 · Live on two phones *(~10s setup)*
> "Enough slides. Let me show you it actually working — on two phones, right now.
>
> *(Hand a phone to a volunteer in the room.)*
>
> You're the customer. I'm the driver."

*(Run the live booking. ~60 seconds.)*

---

### CLOSING — after the live demo *(~90s — say it slowly, this is the ask)*

> "So — why is this going to move fast?
>
> Because it's backed by one of the strongest teams that could possibly be put together for a problem like this. People in this room already know them.
>
> **Alex David Pratt.** **Uncle Rob Pache.** **Mr. Collie.**
>
> People who have shipped much, much bigger systems than this. People you trust.
>
> *(Pause.)*
>
> And this is personal. This is not a deck we put together for a competition. This is something genuinely dear to us. We will stop at nothing — nothing — until the goals we have set with this product are met. The children on that dumpsite will not be there a generation from now. That's the line we've drawn.
>
> *(Pause.)*
>
> And one more thing.
>
> What you are looking at is not just an app. It is the start of a new industry in The Gambia. Because that is how civilization actually works — you build one industry, and it gives birth to a dozen more. You cannot have recycling plants without collection. You cannot have plastic re-manufacturing without sorting. You cannot have a circular economy without the road that gets the waste off the street in the first place.
>
> `mbalit` is that road.
>
> And the same hacker spirit that built this in weeks — that same spirit will give rise to the next product, and the next business, and the one after that. Until we have not only cleaned up our streets — we have reshaped our economy. And, honestly, our society.
>
> *(Pause. Look up.)*
>
> That's what we're here to start. Today. With you.
>
> Thank you."

## Stage directions — cheat sheet

- **Three slowest moments** — they carry the whole pitch:
  1. "Neneh is why we built this."
  2. The five names in Scene 8.
  3. "`mbalit` is that road."
- **Don't rush the stats.** Read each number. Let the audience absorb one before you give them the next.
- **Hand the phone over *before* you start talking** in Scene 20 — physical action sells the demo more than the demo does.
- **The team beat (Alex / Uncle Rob / Mr. Collie)** — say each name on its own line. Look around the room. These are people the audience already trusts; the silence between names does the work.
- **Total runtime** target: 3 min Act I + 2 min Act II + ~3 min Act III (live demo + close) = **≈8 minutes**.
