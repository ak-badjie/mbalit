Yes—beyond the statistics, there are a number of personal, ground-level stories from communities in The Gambia that show how waste dumping and environmental issues affect daily life. 🧍‍♂️ 1. Life Near Dump Sites (Urban Communities)

In areas around Bakoteh Dumpsite (near Serrekunda), residents have described:

Constant smoke from burning waste
Children developing persistent coughs and skin irritation
Families unable to open windows due to the smell

One resident explained that during the rainy season, dirty water mixed with waste flows into homes, increasing mosquito breeding. Parents often link this directly to repeated malaria cases in their children.

👩‍👧 2. Parents Dealing with Recurrent Malaria

In peri-urban communities, especially around flood-prone zones:

A mother reported her child having malaria 3–4 times in one rainy season
She linked it to blocked drains filled with plastic waste, which trap stagnant water

This aligns with broader findings that poor waste management contributes to mosquito habitats—especially for the Anopheles mosquito.

🧹 3. Informal Waste Pickers (“Scavengers”)

At dumpsites like Bakoteh:

Individuals (including teenagers) work daily sorting plastics and metals
Many report no protective gear
Common issues:
Cuts from sharp waste
Respiratory problems from fumes
Occasional infections

Some describe it as “the only way to survive,” even though they are aware of the health risks.

🏫 4. Schools Affected by Nearby Dumping

There have been accounts of schools located near informal dumping zones where:

Students complain of bad odors affecting concentration
Teachers report increased absenteeism due to illness
In some cases, waste accumulates right outside school fences
🌧️ 5. Flooding + Waste = Daily Struggle

During heavy rains:

Residents in low-lying areas report that gutters clogged with plastic overflow
Floodwater carries waste into homes
People wade through contaminated water, sometimes leading to:
Skin infections
Gastrointestinal illnesses

One account described a family spending days cleaning their home after each rainfall because of waste-filled floodwater entering their compound.

🌿 6. Fishermen & Coastal Communities

In coastal areas:

Fishermen have reported catching fish mixed with plastic debris
Some say fish populations seem lower near polluted zones
Concerns about long-term effects on livelihoods and food safety

Stagnant water → mosquito breeding
Mosquitoes → malaria risk

And beyond malaria:

Environmental degradation → economic impact

Said the smell is so bad that even indoors:

Adama Ida Ceesay (landlord)
Reported that sleep becomes difficult at night
Due to flies and pollution from the dumpsite

Mariama Nyassi (waste picker, mother)
Works at the dumpsite collecting plastics and metals

Earns very little (sometimes under D50/day)
Quote: “I earn my income… to feed my family.”
